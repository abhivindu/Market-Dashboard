Latest · 11/02/26Sessions Mon Oct 26 - Mon Nov 2

The Fed held, but kept a December hike in play. On Oct 28, at Kevin Warsh's first meeting as chair, the FOMC kept rates at 3.75%-4.00% by a 10-2 vote; Governor Cook and Cleveland Fed President Hammack dissented in favor of a 25bp hike. Warsh said inflation is too high, that he is 'unwilling and unable' to call financial conditions tight, and that he would not pre-judge meetings. Futures moved to about an 85% chance of a hike at the Dec 8-9 meeting after the press conference (other snapshots put it at ~71-72%), up from ~71% at the Oct 24 pull. The data still runs hot: Q3 GDP grew 3.4% annualized (vs. 3.1% expected) and September PCE inflation was 3.6% y/y, core 3.1%, in line. The October jobs report is due this week (Fri Nov 6).

Yields fell anyway, on flight to safety. The 10-year rose to ~5.05% on Oct 28 after the presser, then fell to ~4.93% on Oct 30, its lowest close since Sept 9, about 7bp below the Oct 24 pull's ~4.99-5.00%. It fell about 36bp in October, its biggest monthly drop since December 2023. The 2-year fell to ~4.56-4.58%, so the curve steepened.

AI earnings broke tech again, and credit followed. Alphabet fell 12.6% on Oct 29 (its worst day since listing) and Intel ~29% on Oct 30, when the Nasdaq dropped 2.86% and the SOX over 7%. The Nasdaq lost 2.0% on the week and about 5% in October, while the equal-weight S&P 500 rose 4.9% for the month. HY spreads widened to 3.44% and IG to 0.94% on Oct 30 (FRED), both the widest in this dashboard's data. On Mon Nov 2 the Nasdaq fell another 1.07% as Marvell (-13%) and Lumentum (-18%) sank on their $115B merger and Oracle (-8%) announced a $20B stock-and-bond raise; the Dow rose 0.41% and most S&P 500 stocks gained. The VIX closed at 21.15.

Oil reversed hard. WTI closed Oct 30 at $104.12, its highest since mid-September, after gaining more than 15% in October. On Nov 2 Brent fell 7.6% to $104.77 and WTI 7.4% to $96.46 on U.S.-Iran peace-talk hopes and a reported Qatar-brokered plan to reopen Hormuz. That explains Monday's selloff in energy-services stocks and the rally in airlines. Gold fell to ~$3,899 on Oct 30 before recovering to ~$3,957 on Nov 2. Data note: Brent's Oct 30 close is reported as both $117.64 and $113.43, likely the expiring December vs. the January contract; Monday's 7.6% drop matches the $113.43 figure. FRED's Nov 2 yield and spread values had not posted on 11/02/26.

Previous · 10/24/26Sessions Mon Oct 19 - Fri Oct 23

Rates fell on fear, not on data. The 10-year Treasury yield dropped about 19bp on Thu Oct 22 to ~4.95%, the biggest one-day fall since August 2025 and the first close under 5% since Sept 23, as money moved into bonds during that day's tech selloff. It ended Friday near 4.99%, down ~11bp on the week (third weekly decline) and below the ~5.10% at the Oct 18 pull. The Fed is in its blackout ahead of the Oct 27-28 FOMC. Odds of an October hike fell from ~15% to roughly 5-10%; a December hike is still priced at ~71%. The data didn't argue for a dovish turn. Jobless claims hit a three-month low (194K), Michigan's year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7%, and the October flash PMI showed output prices rising at the fastest pace since August 2022. Housing is weak: the NAHB index hit 29 (lowest since 2012) and September starts fell 5.9%.

Equities: the chip losing streak ended Monday, but the AI trade broke down again Thursday. Meta fell ~7% after raising capex guidance to $135-145B, Lam Research ~9% on light guidance and Tesla ~3% after a 43% drop in Q3 net income. The SOX fell 6%+, the Nasdaq 2.6% and the S&P 500 1.21% (worst day since July), while the Dow was flat and the Russell 2000 rose. Friday the selling broadened: the Dow fell 1.23%. On the week the S&P 500 was -0.47% and the Nasdaq -0.71% (second straight weekly loss for both); the Dow was +0.41% and the Russell 2000 +2.0%, a rotation out of crowded AI names. The VIX closed at 19.45, below the Oct 16 intraday high of 22.31.

Credit and oil kept tightening the screws. HY spreads widened 13bp to 3.40% on Oct 22 (FRED), concentrated in AI-linked debt: Oracle 5-year CDS reached a record ~315bp on Oct 19, and long-dated Oracle, CoreWeave and Nvidia bonds trade well below par. Brent settled at $116.72 on Fri Oct 23 (six-month high, third weekly gain) and WTI at $103.27, after Houthi strikes on Saudi targets (including a claimed fire at Aramco's Yanbu facility) and Iranian claims of seven tankers hit in Hormuz, of which monitors confirmed two. Gold fell to ~$4,008.

Data note: FRED's WTI series is again out of line with reported settles. It shows $110.01 on Oct 19 and $107.89 on Oct 20, while reported settles were $103.66 and $101.79. The narrative above uses reported prices.

Latest · 10/24/26Sessions Mon Oct 19 - Fri Oct 23

Rates fell on fear, not on data. The 10-year Treasury yield dropped about 19bp on Thu Oct 22 to ~4.95%, the biggest one-day fall since August 2025 and the first close under 5% since Sept 23, as money moved into bonds during that day's tech selloff. It ended Friday near 4.99%, down ~11bp on the week (third weekly decline) and below the ~5.10% at the Oct 18 pull. The Fed is in its blackout ahead of the Oct 27-28 FOMC. Odds of an October hike fell from ~15% to roughly 5-10%; a December hike is still priced at ~71%. The data didn't argue for a dovish turn. Jobless claims hit a three-month low (194K), Michigan's year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7%, and the October flash PMI showed output prices rising at the fastest pace since August 2022. Housing is weak: the NAHB index hit 29 (lowest since 2012) and September starts fell 5.9%.

Equities: the chip losing streak ended Monday, but the AI trade broke down again Thursday. Meta fell ~7% after raising capex guidance to $135-145B, Lam Research ~9% on light guidance and Tesla ~3% after a 43% drop in Q3 net income. The SOX fell 6%+, the Nasdaq 2.6% and the S&P 500 1.21% (worst day since July), while the Dow was flat and the Russell 2000 rose. Friday the selling broadened: the Dow fell 1.23%. On the week the S&P 500 was -0.47% and the Nasdaq -0.71% (second straight weekly loss for both); the Dow was +0.41% and the Russell 2000 +2.0%, a rotation out of crowded AI names. The VIX closed at 19.45, below the Oct 16 intraday high of 22.31.

Credit and oil kept tightening the screws. HY spreads widened 13bp to 3.40% on Oct 22 (FRED), concentrated in AI-linked debt: Oracle 5-year CDS reached a record ~315bp on Oct 19, and long-dated Oracle, CoreWeave and Nvidia bonds trade well below par. Brent settled at $116.72 on Fri Oct 23 (six-month high, third weekly gain) and WTI at $103.27, after Houthi strikes on Saudi targets (including a claimed fire at Aramco's Yanbu facility) and Iranian claims of seven tankers hit in Hormuz, of which monitors confirmed two. Gold fell to ~$4,008.

Data note: FRED's WTI series is again out of line with reported settles. It shows $110.01 on Oct 19 and $107.89 on Oct 20, while reported settles were $103.66 and $101.79. The narrative above uses reported prices. Several CNBC and Bloomberg figures (Oracle/CoreWeave CDS, the PMI) were confirmed from search summaries because the pages blocked access; FRED's Oct 23 credit and yield values had not posted on 10/24/26.

Yields fell back on Oct 15 as investors rotated out of tech into bonds, and on Oct 16 Fed Chair Warsh, speaking in Bangkok, declined to signal October ('I don't prejudge meetings'), cutting hike odds to ~15%. Fed funds are unchanged at 3.75%-4.00%, and futures still price roughly one more hike by December.

Equities set records and then rolled over in tech. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed at records on Oct 6 (7,818.93 and 27,599.79). Since then chips have fallen eight straight sessions, the longest streak since 2008, and the Nasdaq lost 4.5% in the week to Oct 16, its worst week since late June. Friday alone took the Nasdaq down 2.15%, the S&P 500 down 1.09% and the Russell 2000 down 1.66%, while the VIX touched 22.31 intraday. Coverage ties the selloff to a repricing of the AI capex cycle, unwinding of crowded AI positions and AI-debt stress. The Dow held up (+0.67% on the week), helped by rotation into health care, financials and energy.

Credit and oil are the new pressure points. The ICE BofA high-yield OAS has widened further, to 3.23% (about +53bp over the month), with IG at 0.84%, and the stress is concentrated in AI-linked debt. CoreWeave's 5-year CDS hit distress levels (reported near ~1,195bp) on Oct 15, and Goldman flagged a 25bp widening in HY AI-debt spreads over Oct 13-14. Oil reignited on Oct 16: Brent touched $110 for the first time since May and WTI settled at $101.41 (+3.5%) as Iran escalated attacks on Hormuz shipping, at least nine vessels since Oct 11, which is why energy names dominated Friday's gainers. The dollar index is up ~3.5% over the month.

Data note: FRED's WTI series (DCOILWTICO) again looks unreliable. It shows $101.91 on Oct 14, while Reuters-sourced reporting had WTI settling ~$94.14 on Oct 13 and trading ~$94-95 through Oct 14-15. WTI only reached ~$101 at Friday's Oct 16 settle. The narrative above uses independently reported prices, not FRED's levels.

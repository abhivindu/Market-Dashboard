Latest · 10/18/26Sessions Oct 2 - Fri Oct 16

Rates and the Fed whipsawed on data rather than trending. The 10-year Treasury yield is lower than at the Oct 1 pull (5.10% on Oct 15 vs. ~5.2-5.3% around the end of September), but the path was not smooth. September payrolls (released Oct 2) rose just 29,000 vs. ~84,000 expected, with unemployment up to 4.2% and 60,000 of downward revisions, which pulled October-hike odds down to roughly 1 in 5. Then the hotter-than-expected September CPI on Oct 14 (headline 3.7% y/y vs. 3.6% expected; core 2.6% vs. 2.5%, driven largely by a 9.1% jump in gasoline) briefly sent the 10-year to ~5.28% intraday and CME odds of an Oct 27-28 hike to ~54%. Yields fell back on Oct 15 as investors rotated out of tech into bonds, and on Oct 16 Fed Chair Warsh, speaking in Bangkok, declined to signal October ('I don't prejudge meetings'), cutting hike odds to ~15%. Fed funds are unchanged at 3.75%-4.00%, and futures still price roughly one more hike by December.

Equities set records and then rolled over in tech. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed at records on Oct 6 (7,818.93 and 27,599.79). Since then chips have fallen eight straight sessions, the longest streak since 2008, and the Nasdaq lost 4.5% in the week to Oct 16, its worst week since late June. Friday alone took the Nasdaq down 2.15%, the S&P 500 down 1.09% and the Russell 2000 down 1.66%, while the VIX touched 22.31 intraday. Coverage ties the selloff to a repricing of the AI capex cycle, unwinding of crowded AI positions and AI-debt stress. The Dow held up (+0.67% on the week), helped by rotation into health care, financials and energy.

Credit and oil are the new pressure points. The ICE BofA high-yield OAS has widened further, to 3.23% (about +53bp over the month), with IG at 0.84%, and the stress is concentrated in AI-linked debt. CoreWeave's 5-year CDS hit distress levels (reported near ~1,195bp) on Oct 15, and Goldman flagged a 25bp widening in HY AI-debt spreads over Oct 13-14. Oil reignited on Oct 16: Brent touched $110 for the first time since May and WTI settled at $101.41 (+3.5%) as Iran escalated attacks on Hormuz shipping, at least nine vessels since Oct 11, which is why energy names dominated Friday's gainers. The dollar index is up ~3.5% over the month.

Data note: FRED's WTI series (DCOILWTICO) again looks unreliable. It shows $101.91 on Oct 14, while Reuters-sourced reporting had WTI settling ~$94.14 on Oct 13 and trading ~$94-95 through Oct 14-15. WTI only reached ~$101 at Friday's Oct 16 settle. The narrative above uses independently reported prices, not FRED's levels.

Previous · 10/01/26Week of Sept 24 - Oct 1

No new independent shock this week - this is the same rates/inflation throughline from the Sept 16 FOMC continuing to grind, with a notable reversal right at the end. The 10-year Treasury yield kept climbing through Tuesday Sept 29, touching roughly 5.29% intraday (highest since 2007), while the 30-year pushed above 5.6% - its highest since June 2002 - as sticky inflation expectations (the University of Michigan's year-ahead inflation gauge jumped to 4.6% in September from 4.0% in August, the highest since June), heavy Treasury/corporate bond issuance, and a growing federal debt load (near $40 trillion) kept pressure on the long end. That pressure eased abruptly on Wednesday Sept 30 when the PCE inflation print landed below expectations and Fed officials' commentary pulled market-implied odds of an October hike down to under 40% (from the 66-74% priced in as of the Sept 23 pull) - the Nasdaq rallied to close out Q3 on the news.

Credit markets diverged from equities over the same stretch: the ICE BofA High Yield OAS widened roughly 46bps (from about 2.66% on Sept 21 to 3.12% by Sept 30), a five-month high, as a record $38.5B of September high-yield issuance - including SoftBank's $10B bond and Paramount Skydance's $44.4B financing package - tested investors' capacity to absorb new supply even as equities sat near highs; analysts flagged the resulting credit-equity divergence as a risk signal worth watching, not yet a stress event (IG spreads barely moved, and the VIX term structure stayed in calm contango). None of this tripped this dashboard's day-move materiality thresholds the way the last two pulls' sharp single/two-day swings did: over the five trading days through Oct 1 the S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow were all roughly flat-to-down low-single-digits (-0.5%, -0.3%, -0.8% respectively) and the Russell 2000 -1.0% - a genuinely quieter week, not the story disappearing.

Separately on data quality: this dashboard's free FRED oil-price source (DCOILWTICO) printed an implausible run this stretch ($85.23 on Sept 25, $99.37 on Sept 28, $96.16 on Sept 29) that independent sources (Investing.com, Robinhood's WTI prediction-market data) don't corroborate - real WTI traded in a comparatively narrower ~$89-94 band across the same days. Real oil-market volatility was genuinely elevated (the Brent-WTI spread blew out to ~$12.83, its widest since May, as Brent drew a geopolitical premium from stalled Hormuz reopening talks while WTI weakened on US diesel-export-ban talk and rising Saudi/UAE exports), but FRED's specific day-to-day dollar swings over Sept 25-29 look like a data-quality issue rather than a real move of that size - logged in WISHLIST.md, and the 10/01/26 pull's oil-move flag narrative cites the real, independently-reported ~3.5-3.8% WTI decline on Sept 29 rather than FRED's own likely-wrong dollar levels.

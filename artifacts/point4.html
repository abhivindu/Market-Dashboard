Agency RMBS

▸
How it works

Pass-through securities backed by residential mortgages that carry a government or GSE guarantee (Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) against default risk - so unlike every other asset class in this report, credit risk is essentially not the story here. The entire investment case is interest-rate and prepayment risk: when rates fall, homeowners refinance and return principal early (reinvestment risk at lower yields); when rates rise, prepayments slow and duration extends just as rates are moving against you (negative convexity, the defining technical feature of this asset class).

Market size & issuance

MBS issuance reached $337.7B year-to-date through February 2026, up 16.2% y/y; agency MBS trading volume averaged $396.8B daily, up 7.2% y/y - both signal healthy market liquidity and demand.

Performance & spread commentary

Mixed across 2026 so far: the Bloomberg U.S. MBS Index returned +0.07% in April (+0.24% excess return to Treasuries) but -1.42% in July, when sector option-adjusted spread (OAS) widened 7bp to +31bp - a real, sourced spread move, not a proxy. Agency MBS is coming off its best year since 2002 (+8.5% in 2025), and PIMCO's lead portfolio manager describes 2026 valuations as still 'cheap on average' even after that rally.

Institutional commentary

PIMCO (Dan Hyman, managing director): 'We continue to see good opportunities in the agency mortgage in 2026, with valuations on average looking cheap and differentiation of bonds within the market creating good relative value opportunities for active managers' (2026). Separately, PIMCO frames agency MBS as 'an important defensive anchor in the portfolio' given tight corporate spreads elsewhere - a relative-value argument, not an absolute one.

Institutional investment activity

PIMCO's Total Return Fund and Income Fund both hold ~18% in agency MBS as of June 30, 2026. PIMCO's actively-managed MBS ETF (PMBS, $1.3B AUM) drew over $800M in inflows over the trailing year - a clear signal of institutional/advisor rotation into active MBS management specifically, not just passive exposure.

Fed-decision sensitivity

High and structurally amplified by negative convexity. Agency MBS effective duration runs roughly 5-6 years currently, so a 25bp Fed-driven rate move implies a rough 1.25-1.5% price impact before convexity - but because prepayments slow when rates rise, duration extends further right when you don't want it to, meaning realized losses in a selloff tend to exceed the simple duration math, while gains in a rally are capped by refinancing. The Fed's own balance sheet actions matter directly here too: the Fed cut its Reserve Management Purchases to zero starting August 14, 2026, removing a buyer from the market at the margin.

Headwinds & tailwinds

Tailwind: the Trump administration's GSE MBS purchase program (Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac authorized to buy $200B of agency MBS, announced January 8, 2026) tightened spreads and boosted 2026 performance, though PIMCO notes conviction has 'moderated a bit' now that spreads are narrower post-rally. Headwind: continued Fed MBS runoff (holdings fell below $2 trillion by end of Q1 2026) keeps a structural seller/non-buyer in the market with banks 'comparatively inactive,' which is why spreads remain unusually wide by historical standards even with the GSE program's support.

Sentiment & outlook

Constructive but not uncomplicated - PIMCO's own framing captures the tension well: cheap on an absolute/relative basis and a genuine defensive anchor, but the spread-widening episode in July 2026 shows the asset class is still exposed to real volatility when technicals (Fed runoff, bank demand) turn unfavorable even without any credit-quality story at all.

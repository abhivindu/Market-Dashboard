Deep tech · pre-commercial

Quantum Computing

▸
How it works

Uses qubits exploiting superposition and entanglement to explore many computational states at once, in principle solving certain optimization, simulation, and cryptography problems exponentially faster than classical computers. Competing hardware approaches (trapped-ion, superconducting, photonic, neutral-atom) trade off qubit count, error rates, and operating conditions differently.

Maturity

Early commercial, not yet fault-tolerant. 2026 is the first year global quantum-computing revenue crosses $1B (up from $650-750M in 2024), per McKinsey - but this is R&D/access revenue, not mass-market deployment. IBM targets 'practical quantum advantage' by end of 2026 and fault-tolerant computing by 2029; most analysts place widespread commercial applications 5-10 years out.

Applications
  • Portfolio optimization (finance)
  • Molecular/drug-discovery simulation (pharma, chemicals)
  • Post-quantum cryptography migration (cybersecurity)
  • Materials science simulation
Major investors & lenders

Mega-cap strategics (Nvidia's NVentures, Honeywell, Microsoft, JPMorgan, Amgen), sovereign/institutional capital (BlackRock, Temasek, Baillie Gifford), plus the U.S. Commerce Department (direct funding agreements, e.g. Quantinuum's trapped-ion program).

Roadblocks & competing tech

Error correction at scale is the primary unsolved technical barrier - decoherence limits computation to microseconds/milliseconds, and most systems need near-absolute-zero cooling. Real progress exists (Google's Willow chip reached 'below-threshold' error correction; AWS's Ocelot chip targets a 90% cut in error-correction overhead) but the gap between lab demonstrations and deployable systems remains wide. Prediction markets give low odds to IBM's end-2026 advantage claim.

Market sentiment

Volatile and bifurcated, though the public pure-plays have partly rebounded since the late-July trough. UPDATE (this pull): Rigetti and D-Wave each secured $100M in CHIPS Act funding in early September 2026, with the U.S. government taking minority equity stakes in both - shares jumped 8% (RGTI) and 5% (QBTS) on the news, though RGTI and QBTS remain down 26% and 33% year-to-date with the government stakes adding dilution risk. IonQ closed its SkyWater acquisition and raised 2026 revenue guidance to $280-290M, with Remaining Performance Obligations reaching $485M. Private funding remains strong in parallel (PsiQuantum's $1.5B round in May 2026 at a $10.5B valuation; Quantinuum's IPO pricing above a $14B valuation) - institutional conviction hasn't left even as public-market sentiment swings on government-policy headlines as much as fundamentals now. UPDATE (this pull, 2026-09-22): the Department of Energy launched the $215M "Quantum Genesis" Q Competition on Sept 18, a milestone-based program to fund a fault-tolerant, scientifically-relevant quantum computer (100+ logical qubits, useful science by 2028) - Phase I pays up to $1.5M per awardee in fixed milestones, Phase II sets aside $100M for reaching 100 qubits with $50M bonuses at 150 and 200 qubits. IonQ, Rigetti, and D-Wave are all expected entrants; applications close Oct 19, 2026. This is a new, separate federal funding vehicle from the CHIPS Act equity-stake awards above, adding another government-demand catalyst for the pure-plays rather than resolving the sector's underlying government-policy sensitivity.

Physical AI · early commercial

Humanoid & Industrial Robotics

▸
How it works

Bipedal or wheeled general-purpose robots combining vision-language-action (VLA) AI models with precision actuators (often strain-wave/harmonic gears) to perform manipulation and locomotion tasks originally designed for human workspaces - warehouses, factories, eventually homes. The bet is that a human-shaped form factor lets one robot work in infrastructure built for humans without redesigning the environment.

Maturity

Early commercial, moving from staged demos to real pilots. Twelve commercial humanoid platforms became available for purchase or lease in 2026, backed by over $6B in cumulative venture investment; humanoid startups alone raised $8.7B through July 2026, already nearly double all of 2025. Real deployments exist (Figure AI's BMW plant pilot, Agility's Digit in Amazon fulfillment centers, Tesla's internal Optimus use) but are narrow - material handling and parts transfer, not high-speed precision assembly. Broader production-level availability is expected 2027-2028, mass-market comparable to industrial robots today by 2029-2030.

Applications
  • Warehouse/logistics material handling (Amazon, others)
  • Automotive parts transfer and assembly support (BMW)
  • Internal manufacturing use (Tesla's own factories)
  • Future: home/consumer tasks (unproven, furthest out)
Major investors & lenders

Mega-cap strategics (Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon's Industrial Innovation Fund), SoftBank (Masayoshi Son has called robotics the source of the next trillion-dollar company), Jeff Bezos personally, automakers (Hyundai acquired Boston Dynamics outright), and public markets increasingly via IPO/SPAC routes (Unitree's Shanghai IPO, Agility's Churchill Capital SPAC merger).

Roadblocks & competing tech

Investor optimism is outpacing real-world progress - Unitree listed at roughly 210x its 2025 revenue. The harder test now is proving two-legged machines do useful work reliably and cheaply at scale: intervention rates, cost-per-completed-task versus other automation, safety, regulation, and social acceptance remain open questions. Tesla itself tempered expectations in January 2026, acknowledging early Optimus units weren't yet 'doing useful work.'

Market sentiment

Euphoric and clearly ahead of fundamentals in places, but with real differentiation opening up between names. Unitree's Shanghai IPO debut surged as much as 629% (closing +460%) - notable partly because Unitree is actually profitable, a genuine outlier in the category. Boston Dynamics went the acquisition route (Hyundai) rather than a speculative IPO. Agility chose a SPAC at a comparatively modest $2.5B implied valuation - Forbes noted Unitree's pop makes Agility 'look super cheap' by comparison, illustrating how unsettled public-market pricing for the category still is.

Nuclear SMRs

▸
How it works

Small Modular Reactors are factory-fabricated nuclear reactors (typically under 300MW, versus 1,000MW+ for traditional plants) designed to be built in a controlled factory setting and shipped to site, compressing construction timelines from 6-10 years to a targeted 3-5 years. Designs vary (light-water like NuScale/BWRX-300, high-temperature gas-cooled like X-energy, sodium-cooled fast reactors like TerraPower's Natrium), each trading off fuel type, cooling approach, and licensing pathway differently.

Maturity

Pre-commercial in the West. Only four SMR units operate commercially worldwide as of early 2026 (Russia's floating Akademik Lomonosov plant and China's Linglong One demonstration reactor, the latter targeting H1 2026 as the first land-based commercial SMR). In North America, Ontario Power Generation's BWRX-300 is under construction at Darlington targeting 2029 commercial operation - the likely first grid-scale SMR in the region. TerraPower's Natrium in Wyoming targets the early 2030s. A new technology-inclusive NRC licensing framework (Part 53) was finalized in March 2026, but licensing remains slow in practice.

Applications
  • AI data center power (the dominant near-term demand driver)
  • Grid-scale baseload power replacing aging coal/gas plants
  • Industrial process heat
  • Remote/off-grid power (military, mining)
Major investors & lenders

Every major US hyperscaler has signed at least one nuclear offtake deal (Microsoft's 837MW deal among them); the pipeline of conditional offtake agreements grew from 25GW (end of 2024) to 45GW (April 2026). The U.S. DOE is a direct funder (X-energy received an additional $1B in cost-shared funding on top of prior support; TerraPower and others have federal backing too). Private capital includes Bill Gates (TerraPower founder/backer) and specialist energy-transition funds.

Roadblocks & competing tech

Cost is the central problem: Lux Research estimates first-of-a-kind SMRs could run ~$331/MWh versus $124/MWh for natural gas once overruns and delays are factored in. Regulatory/licensing delays are real (an X-energy Texas project recently faced formal opposition on financial-qualification grounds before the NRC's licensing board). HALEU fuel supply is a bottleneck - only one Western producer currently licensed (see Centrus Energy, adjacent). The timeline mismatch is stark: hyperscalers need power now, but even optimistic SMR projections don't see broad deployment before the early 2030s.

Market sentiment

UPDATE (this pull, 2026-09-17): Wall Street itself is now split, not just retail sentiment - Piper Sandler initiated coverage Sept 10 with a Buy on Oklo ($55 target) but a Sell on X-energy ($9 target, ~53% downside), citing Oklo's build-own-operate model versus X-energy's more asset-light approach that leaves customers holding more technology/project risk; UBS separately cut NuScale to Sell on Sept 11 on timeline and cash-burn concerns. Oklo also opened a new $1B at-the-market equity offering on Sept 11 (replacing a prior $1B program that was already fully sold), and shares dipped 3.5-5% on the dilution news even as the company's financing capacity and 14GW customer pipeline both keep growing. The split-the-sector pattern reinforces rather than resolves the divide described below. Prior framing: split between public-market skepticism and continued private/strategic conviction. Oklo and NuScale - the two most-followed public pure-plays - have shed a combined 85.3% in 2026 as investors confront the reality that neither has a revenue-generating project yet, and Bank of America doesn't see real-world adoption meaningfully picking up until 2035-2040. Yet Oklo separately pursued a public listing targeting a $10B+ valuation in February 2026, and hyperscaler offtake commitments keep growing - suggesting strategic/infrastructure investors are underwriting a much longer timeline than public equity holders are currently willing to.

Biotech: GLP-1 Successors & Gene Editing

▸
How it works

Two distinct but adjacent frontiers bundled here. GLP-1 successors work by targeting multiple metabolic hormone receptors at once (dual/triple agonists combining GLP-1, GIP, and amylin pathways) or by reformulating existing peptides as oral small molecules instead of injectables, chasing greater efficacy and convenience than first-generation semaglutide/tirzepatide. Gene editing (CRISPR-Cas9, base editing) directly rewrites disease-causing DNA sequences - Cas9 makes a double-strand cut to disable or replace a gene, while base editing (Beam's approach) rewrites a single genetic letter without cutting the DNA strand, aiming for higher precision.

Maturity

Split maturity within one sector. GLP-1 is fully commercial and already the fourth-largest drug category (~$42B, ~4.6% share), with $132B in 2025 sales (+33.5% y/y) - 2026 is described as the 'year of acceleration' with oral formulations (Lilly's Foundayo, Novo's oral Wegovy) newly launched and triple agonists (retatrutide, 28%+ body weight reduction in trials) in late-stage trials. Gene editing is much earlier: CRISPR Therapeutics' Casgevy is the first FDA-approved CRISPR therapy, but the field overall remains binary-outcome, trial-readout-driven investing - Intellia's lead program isn't expected to launch until 1H 2027 even with FDA priority review.

Applications
  • Obesity/weight management (the dominant GLP-1 use case)
  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Expanding indications: cardiovascular risk reduction, sleep apnea, MASH (liver disease)
  • Gene editing: rare genetic diseases (sickle cell, ATTR-CM, AATD, hereditary angioedema)
Major investors & lenders

GLP-1 leaders are self-funding at scale from existing drug revenue (Lilly, Novo) and increasingly vertically integrating manufacturing (Novo Holdings' $16.5B Catalent acquisition). Gene editing pure-plays rely on public-market capital raises and credit facilities (Intellia's $400M facility for lonvo-z) since none are yet commercially self-sustaining - a fundamentally different capital structure than the cash-generative GLP-1 majors.

Roadblocks & competing tech

For GLP-1: manufacturing capacity has been the binding constraint (driving the Catalent deal and broader CDMO investment), though pricing and insurance/Medicare coverage access remain live policy risks even as they improve. For gene editing: binary clinical/regulatory outcomes - a single trial result or FDA decision can make or break a company - mean risk-averse investors are explicitly advised to avoid the space; only long-horizon, high-risk-tolerance capital is suited to it.

Market sentiment

GLP-1 sentiment is strong but valuation-conscious - Lilly trades at 35x earnings with 'earnings appear to be catching up to the stock's lofty price,' i.e. priced for continued execution, not much room for disappointment. Gene editing sentiment is cautious and increasingly framed as 'what's next after GLP-1' rather than a hyped standalone theme - Motley Fool explicitly pitched CRISPR Therapeutics as an answer to 'what's the next big thing after GLP-1 drugs,' suggesting generalist investors are only beginning to rotate attention there.

▸
How it works

Maturity

Bifurcated: launch and broadband connectivity are genuinely commercial and profitable at scale (SpaceX/Starlink); newer satellite business models (direct-to-cell, dense earth-observation constellations) are earlier-stage with real revenue but not yet consistent profitability. The global space economy hit $470B in January 2026, commercial activity now 78% of total revenue - a mature-enough market that January 2026 marked the first time commercial fully eclipsed government spending as the primary driver.

Applications
  • Satellite broadband internet (Starlink)
  • Direct-to-cell connectivity, no special device needed (AST SpaceMobile)
  • Earth observation - defense, intelligence, agriculture, climate monitoring (Planet Labs)
  • Heavy-lift launch for national security and deep-space missions (Blue Origin's New Glenn, SpaceX)
Major investors & lenders

Roadblocks & competing tech

Vertical integration by the mega-constellation operators (SpaceX, and Amazon's Kuiper) is squeezing independent satellite manufacturers out of addressable market, since value capture concentrates with whoever owns the constellation. Fusion Energy

▸
How it works

Fuses light atomic nuclei (typically hydrogen isotopes) together under extreme heat and pressure to release energy - the same reaction that powers the sun - rather than splitting heavy atoms like conventional fission. Competing confinement approaches: magnetic confinement using high-temperature superconducting (HTS) magnets to contain plasma in a tokamak (Commonwealth Fusion, Tokamak Energy), field-reversed configuration using pulsed magnetic compression (Helion), and other proprietary approaches (TAE's hydrogen-boron aneutronic fuel cycle).

Maturity

Pre-commercial but moving faster than historical fusion timelines suggested - global private investment surged past $13B in 2026, driven substantially by Big Tech's AI-data-center power demand rather than climate policy alone. Commonwealth Fusion installed the first of 18 planned HTS magnets in its SPARC reactor in January 2026 (each magnet: 24 tons, 20-tesla field). Multiple companies claim to be within 18-24 months of demonstrating net energy gain, though under specific experimental definitions, not continuous grid electricity. First commercial plants are projected 2030-2035; Japan has moved its national commercialization target up to the 2030s from the 2040s.

Applications
  • Grid-scale baseload power (the primary long-term target)
  • Direct AI-data-center power offtake (Helion's Microsoft deal is the first concrete example)
  • Hydrogen production and industrial process heat (post-grid-scale maturity)
Major investors & lenders

A mix of Big Tech strategics now investing directly in fusion for the first time (Nvidia joined Commonwealth Fusion's $863M Series B2 alongside Google and Bill Gates's Breakthrough Energy Ventures; OpenAI's Sam Altman personally led Helion's earlier $425M round), plus traditional energy/VC capital (Chevron and Google in TAE's latest round) and - unusually for this sector - a direct merger with a public media company (TAE's all-stock deal with Trump Media & Technology Group, valued above $6B).

Roadblocks & competing tech

Grid connection is a hidden, multi-year bottleneck separate from the physics: an April 2026 grid-connection application was described as just the first step in a process that will take 4-6 years for approval - meaning even a company that solves net energy gain tomorrow can't sell power for years afterward. More broadly, the engineering, materials science, and component durability/economics required after a 'breakthrough' net-energy-gain headline are consistently underestimated relative to the physics milestone itself. Helion's 2028 Microsoft-offtake timeline is described as 'extremely aggressive' even by people sympathetic to its approach.

Market sentiment

Genuine institutional conviction, not pure hype - the presence of Nvidia, Google, Microsoft (as a customer), and Chevron as direct investors/counterparties signals this is being treated as a real infrastructure bet by sophisticated capital, not a speculative retail trade (none of these companies are yet public, so there's no meaningful retail-sentiment signal to track the way there is for quantum or space stocks). The TAE/Trump Media merger is a notable exception - it's the first channel for public-market investors to gain fusion exposure directly, worth watching for how the market prices it once trading begins.

Brain-Computer Interfaces

▸
How it works

Implanted or surface electrodes read electrical signals from neurons and translate them into digital commands, letting the brain directly control a computer, robotic limb, or communication device - bypassing damaged nerve/muscle pathways in people with paralysis, ALS, or other motor/speech impairment. Approaches vary by invasiveness: fully implanted penetrating electrode arrays (Neuralink, Blackrock, Paradromics) offer the highest signal resolution but the most surgical risk; surface/epidural arrays (Precision Neuroscience) trade some resolution for a less invasive procedure; endovascular stent-electrodes threaded through blood vessels (Synchron) avoid open brain surgery entirely.

Maturity

Genuinely early commercial, gated hard by regulation rather than technology. The global BCI market is estimated at $1.4B in 2026, growing to $15.04B by 2035 (16.7% CAGR) - real but still a small market. Crucially, the FDA has not granted full commercial (PMA) approval to any implantable motor-control BCI as of mid-2026; every permanently implanted device aimed at restoring movement or speech remains investigational. The one exception is Precision Neuroscience, which took the less invasive 510(k) regulatory pathway (cleared April 2025) allowing limited commercialization today within a 30-day implant constraint - a meaningfully different (and faster) route to market than Neuralink's or Synchron's.

Applications
  • Communication/computer control for ALS and paralysis (the most advanced use case)
  • Robotic limb/assistive-device control
  • Treatment-resistant depression (newly entered approved commercial deployment)
  • Consumer/non-invasive wearables (gaming, VR, home automation - much lower signal resolution)
Major investors & lenders

Broadening beyond pure medical-device investors into technology and consumer-tech capital - and, notably, crypto capital: Blackrock Neurotech was acquired by Tether (the stablecoin issuer), an unusual entrant into medtech. Synchron's Series D drew sovereign capital (Qatar Investment Authority), a national fund (Australia's National Reconstruction Fund), and existing backers including Bezos Expeditions and ARCH Ventures. Overall 2026 BCI equity funding is down ~25% year-over-year through June, even as individual marquee rounds (Neuralink, Synchron) stayed large - funding is concentrating in the leaders rather than broadening across the field.

Roadblocks & competing tech

Regulatory approval is the central gate: no implantable motor-control BCI has full FDA premarket approval, and Neuralink's initial application was rejected on safety grounds before later clearances. Every company pursuing the traditional implant pathway (Neuralink, Synchron, Blackrock) faces years of pivotal trials before commercial launch, while the 510(k)-pathway approach (Precision Neuroscience) trades regulatory speed for a more limited initial product. Long-term device reliability (electrode degradation, signal drift over years) and cost/reimbursement pathways remain largely unanswered at scale.

Market sentiment

Selectively maturing rather than broadly hyped - a few companies (Neuralink, Synchron) are raising commercialization-scale rounds while the rest of the field remains early, technically fragmented, and dependent on company-specific trial results, per Tracxn's 2026 market read. Neuralink's own valuation trajectory illustrates the uncertainty: a disclosed $9-9.7B primary-round valuation contrasts with unconfirmed secondary-market reports of implied valuations near $40B in early 2026 - a wide gap between official and rumored pricing typical of a hard-to-value, pre-revenue medtech leader.

Next-Gen Batteries & Energy Storage

▸
How it works

Two distinct threads. Solid-state batteries replace a lithium-ion cell's liquid electrolyte with a solid one, promising higher energy density, faster charging, and better safety (no flammable liquid) for EVs - but solid-solid material interfaces degrade mechanically in ways liquid interfaces don't, which is the core unsolved manufacturing problem. Separately, grid-scale storage increasingly uses sodium-ion chemistry (no lithium, cheaper, longer cycle life - CATL cites 15,000 cycles) or lithium-ion in large integrated systems (Fluence), sited behind-the-meter alongside data centers and renewables to solve grid-congestion and interconnection-delay problems that transmission lines can't fix fast enough.

Maturity

Sharply bifurcated, similar to fusion. Solid-state EV batteries remain in pilot-scale purgatory - BYD's sulfide-based pilot plant (Feb 2026, 2GWh) runs below 70% assembly yield versus the 95%+ needed for viability, and true all-solid-state volume production isn't expected before 2028-2030 even by the most aggressive automaker timelines (Toyota 2028, BYD 2030). Grid-scale storage, by contrast, is fully commercial and scaling fast: US utilities plan 24GW of new battery storage in 2026 alone, and CATL's sodium-ion is explicitly declared 'mainstream-ready' with a 60GWh supply agreement already signed.

Applications
  • EV batteries (the solid-state target application, still pre-commercial at scale)
  • Grid-scale storage / renewables firming (fully commercial today)
  • Behind-the-meter data center power (the fastest-growing 2026 use case)
  • Two-wheeler/niche EVs (the only segment with real solid-state product shipping today)
Major investors & lenders

Grid-storage capital has matured into a selective, execution-focused market - investors now reward de-risked, late-stage/operational projects and price speculative ones accordingly, a meaningful shift from earlier hype-driven allocation. Solid-state EV battery makers remain dependent on automaker partnerships and public-market capital (QuantumScape, Solid Power both went public via SPAC/IPO specifically to fund years of pre-revenue R&D) rather than platform-scale venture rounds like fusion or BCI see.

Roadblocks & competing tech

For solid-state: the mechanical interface degradation problem has proven far harder to solve at scale than lab demonstrations suggested, and the 3-5x production cost premium versus conventional lithium-ion makes near-term commercial viability difficult even once the yield problem improves. For grid storage: grid interconnection queues and congestion are now the binding constraint on deployment speed, not battery technology or cost - pushing the industry toward behind-the-meter architectures (up to 30% of planned US data center capacity now includes on-site generation/storage) precisely because waiting for traditional grid upgrades takes too long.

Market sentiment

Split much like fusion and BCI: grid storage sentiment is strong and improving on real execution (Fluence shares jumped 29% in a single session on backlog/margin news), while solid-state EV battery sentiment has soured on repeated timeline slippage - QuantumScape is down 44% YTD as of mid-2026 and trades around $6 versus its $24.80 IPO-era price, with investors now described as watching manufacturing execution rather than revenue, since there essentially isn't any yet.
